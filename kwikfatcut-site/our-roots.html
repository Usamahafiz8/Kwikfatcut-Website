HOME · Our Roots

The old ways were not lost.
They were waiting.

The ancestral food wisdom of Jamaica, West Africa, and the Caribbean — whole, seasonal, local.

The blueprint

Eat what grows naturally, locally, in season.

Modern health troubles built up slowly, as people drifted from natural, seasonal, local whole foods. The original blueprint of human nourishment is whole, indigenous food — seasonal fruit and vegetables, whole grains, legumes, nuts, seeds, and traditional plant proteins.

Caribbean and Ital traditions hold that blueprint still: callaloo, breadfruit, yam, sweet potato, peas and beans, ripe fruit. Natural foods, minimal processing. You get out what you put in.

The line

From West Africa, across the water, into the pot.

So many of the foods on a Jamaican table carry a journey inside them — carried across the water from West Africa, rooted into Caribbean soil, and handed down through generations of hands and pots. The knowledge travelled with the seed.

Three generations, one pot — the whole teaching in one frame.

This is why the work is never only about a single body. It grows in families, in neighbourhoods, in communities that share what they know and hold each other with care. The roots go deeper when the young ones learn what grows here, what it does, and why it matters.
